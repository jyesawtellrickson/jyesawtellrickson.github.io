

When I left my job to pursue my Master's degree part of the reason was because I felt like I had lost the art of thinking deeply. Since graduation I'd spent 10 years working of problems which weren't that HARD. I mean, they weren't easy, but I'd been in small teams optimising for 80% output from 20% of effort and that meant thinking just enough about problems, not as hard as you can. At Meta I had that realisation when I was tasked with working on a well established system that runs billions of actions per day. I needed to think deep. 

As I was leaving my job

One of the biggest sources of power for thinking deeply starts with the most basic idea: focus.  In order to think deeply you should really devote yourself to a problem. That means setting aside focus blocks, defining timeframes for your work, removing notifications.

While focus is important, we still need down time. It's hypothesised that downtime allows our brains to process information in the background. Giving your brain this time can be crucial for synthesising information for insights. In our busy work or endless available distractions it's very easay to jump from one task to another and not give ourselves that rest In the same way, staying healthy and sleeping well don't hurt either.
