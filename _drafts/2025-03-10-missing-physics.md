You likely miss studying physics because it provides a structured, mathematically rigorous way of understanding the world, which AI—especially modern deep learning—often lacks. Here are a few possible reasons for this feeling:

1. Clear First Principles – Physics builds from well-defined axioms and principles (e.g., conservation laws, relativity, thermodynamics), whereas AI often feels more empirical, driven by heuristics and trial-and-error experimentation.


2. Mathematical Rigor – Physics typically relies on elegant, interpretable mathematical frameworks like differential equations, Lagrangian mechanics, and statistical mechanics. AI, particularly deep learning, often involves high-dimensional optimization that lacks the same level of analytical clarity.


3. Unified Theories – Physics seeks grand unified theories that explain multiple phenomena concisely. AI, on the other hand, is fragmented—different architectures and training methods work for different tasks without a unifying theoretical foundation.


4. Physical Intuition – Physics cultivates intuition grounded in reality—forces, fields, and particles behave predictably. In AI, emergent behavior in neural networks can feel unpredictable and unintuitive, making it harder to develop a deep, mechanistic understanding.


5. Experimental Validation – Physics experiments rigorously test hypotheses with controlled conditions, whereas AI research often lacks systematic methodologies, leading to reproducibility issues.



You might enjoy bridging the two fields by exploring AI applications in physics (e.g., differentiable physics, AI-driven simulation, or ML for quantum mechanics). Alternatively, focusing on areas of AI with more mathematical depth (e.g., information theory, probabilistic modeling, or formal verification) could provide a similar intellectual satisfaction.
